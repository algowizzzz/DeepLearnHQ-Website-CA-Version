The five honest struggles — quoted, not paraphrased

Here's the part most course pages will never show you: real criticism from real reviews, and what I did about each.

1. "The platform is too broken" — marketplace problems I couldn't fix

A one-star review from August 2025: "After completing the course I am not getting my certificate and the platform is too broken, it's not updating my progress for 3 weeks. This rating is not for the course but the poor platform." Stuck certificates and lost progress were the most painful category of complaint — and on a marketplace, all I could do was escalate tickets.

What I changed: the bootcamp runs off-marketplace. I set up every student's access personally, the certificate comes from me directly on completion, and if anything breaks you email me — not a ticket queue.

2. "Difficult to follow" — audio, accent and pace

A three-star review from April 2026: "It is clear that the tutor is very knowledgeable but very difficult for me to understand the accent and the sound quality is poor." Another, from June 2026: "Very interesting but speaks too fast, difficult to follow." Fair. Recorded video is one-directional — if my pace loses you, the video doesn't notice.

What I changed: the bootcamp adds the channel recorded video can't offer — a weekly live Q&A where you can ask me to re-explain anything until it lands. Lessons carry closed captions, and the 8-week structure is built for rewatching rather than bingeing.

3. "You have not provided the material" — video alone isn't enough

From May 2026: "You have not provided the material on which you are working." From October 2025: "It should have supporting materials with prompts." The honest lesson: watching someone prompt is not the same as having something to work against.

What I changed: the bootcamp is built around four hands-on projects — each a concrete thing you build against your own job or business, so you're never passively watching. And when you get stuck mid-build, that's exactly what the weekly session is for.

4. "A bit overwhelming" — 14.5 hours is a lot without a plan

From October 2025: "It's a bit overwhelming to attend to all the information shared in the course. I have to rewind some parts." A 179-lecture course rewards structure, and a marketplace gives you none — just a long list of videos.

What I changed: the 8-week path. Foundations before depth, one theme per week, projects spaced so each skill gets used before the next arrives. Self-paced — the weeks are a rhythm, not a deadline.

5. "We can't practice without the pro version" — tool costs

From January 2026: "There are things we cannot practice because we don't have the pro version of ChatGPT." Real concern, honest answer: there is a $0/month path through the entire bootcamp. I show you which free tiers genuinely cover the work and where paying is actually worth it — most students never need a subscription to finish.
