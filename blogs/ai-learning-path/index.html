What should I learn first in AI?

(The #1 question — and the one most people get backwards.) Learn to use AI before you learn about it. The single highest-leverage first step is opening one tool and running a task you already do at work or school through it today. Theory, model names, and "how LLMs work" can wait — they make far more sense after you've felt what the tools do. So the path below is ordered by what pays off soonest, not by what a textbook would cover first.

The 30-day AI learning path

Days 1–7 — Pick one tool and use it daily

Choose one of ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini (not all three — pick one and go deep). Each day, hand it one real task: rewrite an email, summarise a document, plan your week, explain something you don't understand. The goal isn't output quality yet — it's reps. Not sure which to pick? Use our "which AI should you use?" picker or the best AI chatbot comparison. The free tier is more than enough this week.

Days 8–16 — Learn to prompt (the one skill that compounds)

Now add structure. The biggest jump in quality comes from one habit: frame requests as Role + Task + Context + Format ("You are a recruiter. Rewrite this résumé bullet. Context: applying for a marketing role. Format: three punchy options."). Same free tool, dramatically better output. Practice with real examples from ChatGPT prompts that actually work and keep a personal swipe file using our free AI prompt library. If you want the deeper version, see prompt engineering.

Days 17–30 — Ship one real project

Pick one outcome that matters to you — automate a weekly report, write a landing page, build a study guide for an exam, draft a month of social posts — and take it end to end with AI as your co-pilot. This is the step that turns "I've tried ChatGPT" into a skill you can point to. A finished project beats ten more explainer videos, and it's exactly what employers (and your own confidence) respond to.

What to skip (for now)

  • Maths and Python — only needed to build models, not to use AI. Skip unless that's your goal.
  • Model theory and benchmarks — interesting later; useless before you've used the tools.
  • Collecting certificates — one recognized cert tied to a real project beats a wall of badges. See the best AI certifications for which actually carry weight.
  • Chasing every new tool — go deep on one, then branch. Depth first, breadth later.

Where to go after the 30 days

Once the basics are automatic, branch toward your goal: a structured generative AI course to go deeper, the best free AI courses if you're on a budget, the AI skills that actually matter for your role, or AI agents if you want AI to take actions, not just answer. Whichever you pick, the muscle you built in month one — use, prompt, ship — is what carries you.
