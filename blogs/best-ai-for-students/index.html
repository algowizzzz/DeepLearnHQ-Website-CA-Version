What is the best AI for students?

(The #1 question.) If you want one answer: start with ChatGPT (free). It's the most forgiving all-rounder for studying, explaining tricky concepts, quizzing you, and giving feedback on your writing. But "best" really depends on the job — so match the tool to the task below, and remember the free tiers are genuinely enough for schoolwork.

📚 Best for studying & understanding — ChatGPT (free)

Ask it to explain a concept "like I'm 15," generate practice questions, or turn your notes into a quiz. Used as a patient tutor, it's the single most useful study tool a student can add. The right way to do this — learn, don't outsource — is in our guide to ChatGPT for students.

✍️ Best for essays & long readings — Claude

Claude handles long documents and nuanced writing feedback especially well — paste a reading and ask for a summary, or your draft and ask for structural critique (not a rewrite). See the head-to-head in Claude vs ChatGPT.

🔎 Best for research with sources — Perplexity

When you need current, citable answers rather than a confident guess, Perplexity is built for it — every answer comes with clickable sources you can verify (and cite properly). More options in the best AI tools.

📄 Best if your school uses Google — Gemini

If your assignments live in Google Docs and Drive, Gemini is the least-friction pick. Not sure which to commit to? Our "which AI should you use?" picker matches a tool to your task in ten seconds.

The rule that keeps you out of trouble

AI is a tutor, not a ghostwriter. Use it to understand, quiz yourself, brainstorm, and edit your own work — never to submit AI-written work where original work is required. FAQ

What is the best AI for students? ChatGPT's free tier as an all-rounder; Claude for essays and long readings; Perplexity for cited research; Gemini if your school uses Google. Is there a free AI that's good enough? Yes — the free tiers cover almost everything a student needs. Will using AI get me in trouble?
