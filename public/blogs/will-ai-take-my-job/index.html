Will AI actually take my job?

The honest answer for most roles: augmentation, not replacement. AI is very good at tasks (drafting, summarising, first-pass analysis) and much weaker at whole jobs, which need judgement, context, relationships, and accountability. What's really happening is that AI absorbs the repetitive parts, and the human moves up to the parts that need a human. The jobs at genuine risk are the ones that are only repetitive tasks.

Which jobs change most (and least)

  • Most exposed: highly repetitive, language- or data-heavy tasks — basic copywriting, data entry, first-line support, routine reporting.
  • Least exposed: work needing physical presence, deep trust, complex judgement, or human relationships — but even these get an AI-assisted upgrade.
  • The pattern: almost no job vanishes entirely; almost every job changes. The tasks shift, the tool-use expectation rises.

The real risk isn't AI — it's someone using AI

Employers aren't replacing analysts with ChatGPT; they're expecting analysts to use ChatGPT and get more done. The divide forming in every field is between people who can direct these tools and people who can't. That's good news, because the skill is learnable in weeks, not years.
